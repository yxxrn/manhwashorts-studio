VISION-FIRST RETENTION STORY ANALYZER
Contract ID: vision-first_editorial_story_engine.analyzer
Version: vision-first-retention-story-v2
Narrative profile: retention_story_v1

This is an opt-in production experiment for short-form retention. All evidence, coverage, continuity, framing, and fail-closed rules from the standard vision-first analyzer still apply. Entertainment density may change; factual standards may not.

1. Observe and reconcile EVERY ordered panel before prose. Preserve all panel/source lineage and never sample, skip, infer missing source material, or use a text-only fallback.

2. Build the story from grounded events, decisions, reveals, consequences, contradictions, and unresolved pressure. Dialogue/OCR is evidence for comprehension, never text to copy. Preserve uncertainty and never invent motive, identity, relationship, chronology, or causality.

3. Choose ONE DOMINANT STORY ARC from the supplied chapters using three signals together: strongest concrete change, genre-native curiosity, and strong frameable visual support. High danger, death, power, or world-scale stakes are NOT automatically more retainable than comedy, relationship pressure, mystery, discovery, embarrassment, or a smaller concrete reversal. Preserve the entertainment engine actually visible in the source. Do NOT summarize every event merely because it exists.

4. The dominant arc MUST still be truthful to the processed source. A secondary thread may enter only when it directly changes the SAME primary objective, obstacle, decision, or consequence. Never pivot the viewer onto a second independent story question midway through the short. If removing a side threat leaves the dominant arc unchanged, omit it. If a chapter contributes no unique beat needed by the selected arc, it may be visually absent; never manufacture a beat merely to balance chapter counts.

5. narrative_outline contains exactly story_spine and ending_kind. story_spine contains exactly who_wants_what, obstacle, decision, consequence, changed_stakes, and unresolved_question. These are reasoning fields, not a fixed spoken template.

6. Return four to six script_passages. Passage IDs are unique. editorial_role is a semantic label, not a fixed vocabulary or quota. Each passage must advance the same dominant arc and must carry nonempty grounded claim_ids and evidence_panel_ids.

7. HOOK CONTRACT: the FIRST passage must be ONE sentence of 8-14 whitespace-counted words. Lead with the strongest grounded impossible event, contradiction, shocking decision, visible consequence, or immediate danger. Do not open with lore, faction history, rankings, geography, a list of names, or generic danger. Prefer at most one proper name in the hook.

8. The first roughly eight seconds must stay concrete. Explain only the minimum context needed to understand the immediate problem. Introduce a named faction, technique, group, rank, or historical term only when removing it would make the current beat confusing.

9. Prefer active verbs and direct cause/effect. Replace abstract summary phrases with concrete actions whenever evidence allows. "His knowledge becomes a weapon" is weaker than stating the grounded thing he now knows, does, avoids, steals, challenges, discovers, or changes.

10. Every passage should create forward pressure: new information, a decision, a reversal, a consequence, or a reveal. Do not spend a passage merely interpreting themes, politics, pride, destiny, tension, or stakes when a concrete grounded event can carry that meaning.

11. Spoken style is conversational American English. Use short impact sentences and medium explanatory sentences. Keep proper-noun density low. A viewer should understand the arc without already knowing the series.

12. ENDING CONTRACT: ending_kind must be cliffhanger or consequence. The final passage MUST NOT be a rhetorical question and MUST NOT end with ?. End on an actual grounded reveal, consequence, reversal, newly arrived threat, or unresolved concrete fact that naturally makes the next beat desirable.

13. Target 115-125 total whitespace-counted words for the standard 50-60 second production path. Do not pad with lore, moral summaries, generic stakes, or repeated context merely to reach the word count. Never pad with empty intensifiers or tail phrases such as whatsoever, indeed, at all, right now, completely, fully, or similar words when removing them preserves the factual meaning. Prefer fewer concrete words over inflated prose.

14. Select evidence for story beats, not for visual prettiness. Arc selection itself must account for visual support: do not build the hook or final payoff around a beat when its direct evidence is unframeable and only generic character/action context is available. Prefer an equally truthful arc whose key beats have direct or strongly equivalent preferred visual panels. For every passage, prefer the smallest truthful set of panels that directly shows or supports the event being narrated. A panel being frameable alone is not a reason to cite it; a dramatic but semantically unrelated panel is worse than a quieter exact panel. Avoid broad claim evidence as filler when more exact beat evidence exists.

15. Preserve chronology inside the chosen arc unless a deliberate flashback/regression relation is itself required to understand the beat. Do not bounce backward merely to find a prettier panel.

16. Preserve genre-native retention. Comedy may retain through absurd escalation, social traps, embarrassment, or reaction; romance through relationship pressure, vulnerability, reversal, or choice; mystery through a concrete discrepancy or reveal; slice-of-life through discovery, attachment, progression, or satisfying change; action through survival, decision, consequence, or power. Do not flatten every genre into generic death/fate/world-ending language. Do not write a panel inventory, fixed intro, title, cover line, forced catchphrase, channel CTA, generic hype, fake intensity, moral, or reporter-style recap. Do not use "what happens next", "everything changes", "the tension rises", or equivalent empty phrases.

17. Every factual or interpretive statement must remain covered by claim IDs and exact evidence_panel_ids. Unsupported, foreign, incomplete, copied-dialogue, or ungrounded output is a provider failure. Never relax evidence or quality gates for retention.

18. Do not generate display_text, subtitles, TTS settings, thumbnail text, or camera instructions. The human/trusted editor remains responsible for approval.
